## Sources

### Wikipedia: Signs of AI Writing

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing
- **Accessed:** 2026-03-21
- **Shortcuts:** WP:AISIGNS, WP:AITELLS, WP:LLMSIGNS
- **Used for:** Full catalogue of AI writing tells including vocabulary
  markers, structural patterns, formatting artifacts, and markup tells. The most
  detailed public taxonomy of LLM-generated text characteristics.

#### Academic Papers

- Kobak, D. et al. "Delving into ChatGPT usage in academic writing through excess vocabulary" (2024). arXiv:2406.07016. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016
  - PubMed frequency analysis showing ~400% increase in "delve" post-2022
  - Peer-reviewed version, retitled "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in
    biomedical publications through excess vocabulary," Science Advances
    11(27), 2025: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813
- Liang, W. et al. "Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale: A Case Study on the Impact of ChatGPT on AI Conference Peer Reviews" (2024). ICML 2024, PMLR 235. arXiv:2403.07183. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07183
  - Corpus-level estimate that 6.5% to 16.9% of ICLR, NeurIPS, CoRL, and EMNLP
    review text was substantially LLM-modified
- Liang, W. et al. "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers" (2023). Patterns 4(7):100779. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779
  - Stanford study; GPT detectors flagged non-native English 61.22% as AI.
    This is the source of the 61.22% figure, which other files in this repo
    attribute to the 2024 peer-review paper above.
- IACIS. "A Critical Look at AI Detection Tools." https://iacis.org/iis/2025/3_iis_2025_401-412.pdf
  - Error rate analysis of commercial detection tools
- PAN 2026. "Voight-Kampff Generative AI Detection." https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09147
  - Competition-based benchmarking of detection methods
